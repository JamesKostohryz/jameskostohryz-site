James A. Kostohryz is an independent investment strategist who has spent nearly thirty years in global markets — first inside major investment banks and asset-management firms, and since then as an independent, running his own research and consulting practice.

His specialty is where most generalists are thinnest: emerging-markets and Latin American equities, the banking sector, and energy — read through a global-macro and geopolitical lens. He trades the consequences of world events through specific instruments, and he prizes intellectual honesty: he separates what he knows from what he infers, flags his own errors, and states out loud what would prove a thesis wrong.

A record of seeing it early

Over more than 1,000 published articles, Kostohryz built a reputation for calling major turns ahead of the crowd — warning of the 2008 Global Financial Crisis and then calling the recovery of US banks off the bottom; flagging the European sovereign-debt crisis; anticipating the COVID demand shock and the crash that followed; and reading the market consequences of Russia's invasion of Ukraine. His method is not prophecy. It is a repeatable way of finding what the marginal buyer and seller are failing to price, and sizing the bet to the reward-to-risk.

The author's dimension

That instinct became a book. In Year's Purchase: A History of How Income and Profit Have Been Priced, Kostohryz traces the long history of valuation — how, across centuries, investors have put a price on income and profit, and what that history teaches about the mistakes markets keep repeating. The book's through-line runs straight into his working framework today: the AEG method — Abnormal Earnings Growth — which ties the major valuation approaches together with rigor rather than hand-waving.

Independent by design

After years inside large institutions, Kostohryz chose independence deliberately. It lets him write what the analysis says — non-partisan, unafraid of consensus, and accountable only to the reader. He treats his subscribers as a peer group, not an audience: the goal is to teach people how to see the market, hold their nerve through violent moves, and manage risk so they can stay with a thesis long enough to be right.

He was educated at Harvard Law School (JD), Stanford University (BA, Economics, with Honors), and the University of Oxford (Philosophy, Politics & Economics).

"Keep your exposure to a level such that even if the market goes violently against you, you can afford to absorb it. That is what lets you hold the thesis through to the payoff."
