The Method

A system, not a set of opinions.

Most market commentary is a stream of takes. My work rests on a repeatable, top-down process with four components — read the business cycle, price relative value across currencies, value individual assets, then build and risk-manage the portfolio.

Component one · Macro regime

Business Cycle Analysis

Where we sit in the business cycle is the single biggest driver of medium-term returns. I track the U.S. and global cycle — the sequence from expansion to slowdown to recession to recovery — to anticipate turning points before they show up in the data. This sets the regime for everything that follows, and it is the discipline behind calling recessions, recoveries, and the bear markets and corrections that travel with them.

Component two · Relative value

Currency Valuation

Currencies swing in long cycles around fundamental fair value. I estimate that fair value from fundamentals and trade the deviations — using currency exposure both as a direct source of return and as a lever on the value of every other asset in a global, multi-asset portfolio.

Component three · Intrinsic value

Asset Valuation

Every company has an intrinsic value — and a market price that implies a specific set of expectations about the future. My Real Value Analysis (RVA) framework restores economic meaning to the financial statements, values each business through the method its economics call for, and reverse-engineers the expectations the market has priced in.

Component four · Portfolio construction

Asset Allocation, Security Selection & Risk Control

The first three components feed portfolio construction. Capital is allocated across non-correlated factors; individual securities are selected on their reward-to-risk; and risk is controlled at both the position and portfolio level — low individual-position VaR, uncorrelated factors, and a deliberately low beta to the U.S. market late in the cycle.

How they come together

Four components, one portfolio

These aren't separate opinions — they're integrated, and they run top-down. The business cycle sets the regime; currency and asset valuation locate the opportunities within it; and allocation, selection, and risk control turn those opportunities into a portfolio you can actually hold through violent moves. That integration is what the daily and weekly reports apply to live markets.
