Real Value Analysis (RVA)

A practical, intuitive and rigorous framework for estimating a company's intrinsic value — and for reverse-engineering the marginal expectations about fundamentals that are implicit in the current market price.

The core idea

Value is created by economic profitability. Valuation methods that fixate on finding "cheap" stocks or chasing "growth" are focused on the wrong things. What actually matters is a business's capacity to earn returns — on its current stock of invested capital, and on the incremental capital it invests — that exceed its cost of capital.

There is a big problem. The financial statements of every single company are distorted — by inflation, and by accounting rules that misstate the value of a firm's invested capital and change the meaning of profit in ways that are misleading.

You cannot value what you cannot measure.

Real Value Analysis is designed to restore economic meaning to financial statements — so they accurately measure the capital a firm has actually invested, and so they consistently account for what truly counts as a profit or a loss. Only on that foundation can intrinsic value be estimated with confidence, and the marginal expectations embedded in the market price be reverse-engineered.

From measurement to forecasting

Once historical financial statements have been restored to economically sound values, the real work begins: forecasting the value a company will create — or destroy — in the future.

Firms differ enormously across industries. A bank is not a software firm; a pipeline is not a biotech. The appropriate forecasting and valuation model is simply the one that gives the clearest perspective on the value-creation process of the specific company in question. That is why RVA adopts, modifies, and integrates five core methods and four supplementary ones — to cover the full range of companies operating in very different environments.

Here is the part that surprises people. The value of running multiple methodologies is not in the intrinsic-value estimates they produce:

All nine methods are mathematically reconciled to six digits — given identical inputs, they return the exact same estimate of intrinsic value.

The point is different. Each methodology is a different way of looking at a company, and each tends to lead the same analyst toward different inputs. By running up to nine models at once, the analyst gains multiple vantage points from which to assess how a firm creates value — and its capacity to generate incremental real value in the future.

Five core methods, plus four supplementary

The five core methods

  • Abnormal Earnings Growth (AEG)
  • Abnormal Operating Income Growth (AOIG)
  • Residual Income Valuation (RIV)
  • Abnormal Dividend Growth (ADG)
  • Net Asset Value (NAV)

The four supplementary methods

  • Cash Flow Return on Investment (CFROI)
  • Real Options Valuation (ROV)
  • Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE)
  • Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF)

Coverage share doesn't equal importance: the supplementary methods serve as primaries in specific sectors and as cross-checks everywhere.

What each method is for

Abnormal Earnings Growth (AEG) / Abnormal Operating Income Growth (AOIG)59%20%The primary default across asset-light operating companies (tech, media, healthcare, retail, services). Abnormal Dividend Growth (ADG)6%15%Primary for dividend aristocrats, regulated utilities, and mature cash cows, where the dividend serves as an economically appropriate valuation anchor.
Net Asset Value (NAV)4%10%Primary for passive real estate, unmined land, and liquidation scenarios; an asset floor across REITs, reserves, mining, and heavy manufacturing.
Cash Flow Return on Investment (CFROI)14%27%Primary for capital-intensive sectors (industrials, utilities, telecom, autos, chemicals), where physical replacement cost dictates real return on capital.
Real Options Valuation (ROV)2%3%Primary for early-stage biotech R&D and unexplored energy / mining tracts, valued on managerial flexibility and contingent outcomes.
Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE)——Provides cross-validation of the other methods through a focus on cash conversion and generation.
Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF)——Provides cross-validation of the other methods by tracking the flow of cash throughout the entire enterprise.

Primary / Secondary = approximate share of coverage where the method leads or serves as the cross-check. FCFE and FCFF are held as DCF cross-checks rather than primary methods.
