Does the strategy do what it says?

Three objectives, three tests, measured over the whole record from 31 December 2018. Each is scored against the benchmark the strategy assigns to it — not against whichever comparison happens to flatter it. The definitions of the three benchmarks are directly below.

Performance

How this portfolio is benchmarked

The three reference series on this page do three different jobs. They are not interchangeable, and the strategy is not managed against all three.

Relative benchmark 60 / 40 balanced portfolio iShares AOR, total return. This is the relative bar. Beating it is the portfolio's relative-return objective.
Absolute benchmark Treasury bills plus a real equity premium The realized T-bill yield plus roughly 4.5% real per year — the long-run fundamental equity premium. Clearing this over a full business cycle, without large drawdowns, is the definition of success.
Stress reference — not a benchmark S&P 500 Used to define the market-decline windows against which downside protection is measured, and as the reference for beta and correlation. It is not a performance yardstick: the book runs hedged and cash-heavy and is built to be uncorrelated, so lagging the index in a melt-up is expected and on-strategy. It is shown throughout for context, never as the bar.
