Abnormal Operating Income Growth (AOIG)

The enterprise-level member of the abnormal-growth family within Real Value Analysis — the whole-firm counterpart to equity-level Abnormal Earnings Growth (AEG).

On this page

  • Overview

Overview

Abnormal Operating Income Growth (AOIG) applies the abnormal-growth logic at the enterprise level. Where Abnormal Earnings Growth (AEG) works directly on the earnings that accrue to equity holders, AOIG works on operating income to the whole firm — valuing the entire enterprise from the growth in operating income above a normal, required level, then bridging to equity value by adjusting for debt and other claims.

Where it fits in RVA

Within Real Value Analysis, AOIG is the primary enterprise-level method for asset-light, intangible-driven businesses (software, technology, media, mature pharma) and for mature cash-cow / dividend-aristocrat companies — the segments where whole-firm operating economics are cleaner to model than reconstructed book value. It pairs with equity-level AEG (or ADG for dividend-driven names) to give a two-sided read on value.

AOIG vs. AEG

AEG and AOIG are the same idea at two levels. AEG operates on earnings to equity; AOIG operates on operating income to the whole firm — which sidesteps distortions from capital structure and is often the cleaner lens for enterprise comparisons and for companies where financing is complex. Given identical inputs the two reconcile to the same value; their worth is as two different vantage points that lead the analyst toward better-informed inputs.
