The reprieve is real — the crisis clock is only paused

Wk ending Jul 10 · Timed to EIA · Independent, fundamentals-first
Executive read. This week reinforces the desk's "rented, not owned" stance. US crude drew a modest 1.7 mmbbl and a fresh geopolitical bid lifted Brent back to ~$88 / WTI ~$82 — but the tape is being driven by Hormuz risk, not by crisis-pace draws. Base case: draws resume at ~5 mb/d after July; wide distribution around it, gated almost entirely by Hormuz flows and incoming ballast-tanker traffic. We are not chasing this rally; we respect the tail.

Refining margins — 3-2-1 crack

The 3-2-1 crack — (2 × 42 × RBOB + 42 × heating oil − 3 × WTI), the margin from turning three barrels of crude into two of gasoline and one of distillate — is the cleanest real-time read on product tightness and refining demand. It has blown out: the front basket printed ≈ $211 per 3-bbl (≈ $70/bbl), versus a ~$20–30/bbl norm. A margin that extreme is refiners bidding aggressively for crude, and it corroborates the inventory picture — tight gasoline and lean distillate. Rising cracks pull crude up; a crack this stretched also flags eventual demand rationing if it persists.

Demand

  • China — the wildcard. Imports collapsed −4.76 (May) / −6.4 (June) mb/d on price-control-driven refinery-run cuts; the desk sees upside skew (China likely draws SPR and lets product prices rise to restart runs). Any rebound is a direct hit to OECD stocks.
  • Asia ex-China restocking off released Gulf floating storage; +1.5–3 mb/d of incremental demand returns as inventories normalize. Korea KOGAS gas inventories at a 5-yr low — an LNG-side stress marker.
  • US products — gasoline tight, distillate softening; net demand read is mixed, not booming.
