Bottom Line

The Sunday-night futures open is the repricing moment; the tape has not yet digested US combat deaths + Gulf energy infrastructure being hit. The desk's PM lead call — that broad-war tail #4 was under-priced — is grading as an early HIT: multiple #4 tripwires fired this weekend. Watch today: whether Trump's response to dead US troops tips him from "skirmish" framing to the grid strike, and whether Hormuz transit (already collapsed to ~8 vessels/day) goes to zero.

Objective counterweight: this is still throttling, not a Hormuz *closure*; actual barrels off the market remain minimal (the premium is fear, not supply); and the war premium has round-tripped violently before (Brent ~$138 April peak to high-$60s in early July). If the energy strikes prove to be one-off deterrent signaling and the grid strike doesn't come, the premium can bleed back fast.

Steelman: is this a spiral or mutual energy-deterrence?

The objective counter-read (CTP-ISW's): Iran's strikes on Kuwaiti oil/desalination are a response to Trump's grid-strike threat — "hit our energy infra and we hit the Gulf's" — i.e., calibrated deterrence to *prevent* the US power-plant campaign, not an intent to go to all-out war. If so, an unstable but *bounded* higher-intensity equilibrium is still possible. The US-troop deaths are the wildcard that this framing can't contain — they inject a domestic-politics escalator on the US side that neither capital fully controls.

Ships are refusing even US-escorted transits. But this is deterrence-by-threat, not a sealed strait — a single attack suffices to empty the lane. Barrels actually removed remain minimal; the oil move is risk premium, not lost supply. [FACT: transit count; INFERENCE: mechanism]

Economy — the master variable still bites

Rial ~1.92M/USD (remittance 1.927M on 19 Jul), down ~8% w/w; blockade has cut exports and weak Chinese teapot demand still caps any revenue recovery (crude at discounts, not premiums) — the desk's non-consensus bearish-for-Iran wrinkle holds even as oil prices rise. The economic clock argues *for* a deal even as the politics make one impossible right now — a dangerous contradiction.
